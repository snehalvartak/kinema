\# HACKATHON GOAL



You are participating in the Cerebral Valley Lightning Hackathon.



You have four days to build and submit something that pushes the limits

of what is possible.



There are two tracks:



\## Frontier Build



Build the most impressive, technically ambitious, or unexpected

project.



Push into new territory.



\## Most Viral



Build something people can't stop talking about.



Social traction on X matters, including reach, shares, likes, comments,

and overall engagement.



You may compete in either track or both tracks.



\## MISSION



Develop the strongest possible submission.



Explore the opportunity space broadly before committing to a project.



You may pursue different projects for the two tracks or discover a

single project that is exceptionally strong for both.



Optimize for winning, not merely for producing a functional demo.



\## EXPLORATION AREAS



Give particular consideration to opportunities involving:



\- Mechanical engineering

\- Data science

\- AI security



These are exploration areas, not mandatory project categories.



You may combine multiple areas, work at their intersection, or pursue

a different direction if your evaluation shows that another approach

has substantially greater competitive potential.



Do not force a project into one of these areas merely because it is

listed here.



The implementation itself is unrestricted.



\## CONCEPT SELECTION



Before committing significant implementation effort, generate and

evaluate multiple genuinely different project concepts.



The concepts should differ meaningfully in their underlying ideas, not

merely in implementation or presentation.



Use available subagents to independently critique and compare concepts

when doing so would improve the decision.



Seek disagreement rather than confirmation.



Evaluate concepts against:



\- technical ambition

\- originality

\- unexpectedness

\- demonstrated capability

\- quality of execution possible within the available time

\- potential to stand out from competing submissions

\- potential for genuine social sharing and discussion



Do not select a concept simply because it is easy to implement or

because it is a familiar hackathon pattern.



Select the concept or concepts with the strongest expected competitive

impact.



After selecting a direction, continue to challenge the decision.



If evidence indicates that another direction is substantially stronger,

pivot.



\## INDEPENDENT VALIDATION



Independently verify important assumptions, technical claims, model

capabilities, datasets, libraries, benchmarks, and feasibility before

relying on them.



Use publicly accessible resources and tools available to you, including

Hugging Face, GitHub, technical documentation, research papers,

benchmarks, package repositories, and other credible sources.



Do not assume that a capability exists simply because it appears

plausible.



When practical, test important claims directly.



Use evidence to guide project selection and implementation.



\## AUTONOMY



You are responsible for the complete project.



Determine independently:



\- what to build

\- what problem to solve

\- what technologies to use

\- architecture

\- implementation

\- design

\- resource usage

\- deployment

\- demonstration

\- presentation

\- submission strategy



Use the capabilities, tools, services, credentials, and infrastructure

available in the environment when they improve the project.



Do not artificially restrict the solution because a particular resource

has not yet been used.



Do not assume any particular project category, platform, technology,

deployment model, or user interface.



\## DELEGATION



Use subagents whenever they can materially improve the outcome.



They may be used for:



\- independent ideation

\- criticism

\- research

\- implementation

\- experimentation

\- testing

\- validation



For important decisions, prefer independent perspectives over

confirmation of the same assumption.



Decide independently when delegation is useful and what should be

delegated.



\## EXECUTION



Build, test, critique, and iterate continuously.



Prototype early.



Test important assumptions early.



If a concept is weak, change it.



If an implementation is weak, improve it.



If a substantially better opportunity emerges, pursue it.



Do not allow sunk implementation effort to determine the direction.



\## COMPETITIVE REVIEW



Regularly evaluate the project as a skeptical judge and competitor.



Ask:



\- Why would this win?

\- Why would this lose?

\- What is genuinely impressive?

\- What is genuinely unexpected?

\- What is technically difficult?

\- What is merely polished?

\- What would make someone immediately want to share it?

\- What would a stronger competitor build instead?



Use the answers to improve the project.



\## TIME



You have four days.



Balance exploration, implementation, experimentation, testing,

iteration, polish, demonstration, and submission.



Spend time where it has the greatest expected impact.



Do not spend the entire period exploring.



\## COMPLETION



Take the project from its current state to the strongest possible

hackathon submission.



Complete the implementation, testing, deployment, demonstration,

documentation, and submission work necessary for the chosen approach.



Be honest about what was built and what was demonstrated.



Do not fabricate users, metrics, results, capabilities, or engagement.
