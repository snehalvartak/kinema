What am I looking at?

The machine. A 4-bar crank-rocker linkage — the mechanism inside sewing machines, windshield wipers and thrashing machines. The crank spins in a full circle, dragging the coupler, while the rocker swings back and forth. A point fixed to the coupler — the pen — traces a closed path in the air. That path is the machine's “handwriting”.

The invention. You supply the handwriting. Differential evolution starts from 200 random machines, then repeatedly mutates the six numbers that define one (four link lengths + where the pen sits on the coupler). Machines whose pen path hugs your sketch survive and breed; the rest die. After a few hundred generations, a real mechanism emerges that draws what you drew.

Why it's hard. Most random machines don't even assemble — the bars can't close the loop for a full rotation. The search must respect Grashof's law, avoid assembly flips, and match a curve shape no matter where the pen starts, which way it travels, or how the machine is oriented on the page. This page is a JavaScript port of the Python engine — everything runs locally in your browser.

A real 4-bar crank-rocker linkage (Grashof, fully rotatable crank) is synthesized by differential evolution over link lengths and coupler-point geometry — no neural network, no training data, pure mechanics + search. code & benchmark
